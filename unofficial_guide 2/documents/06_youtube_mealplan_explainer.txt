Source: YouTube video by "peace aroibeke" — meal plan explainer for incoming Howard freshmen (rising junior psychology major at the time)

How the meal plan system works:
The meal plan shows up on your Bison bill along with tuition and housing. The price varies for each meal plan. When you go somewhere to eat, staff swipe your Bison One Card or tap the chip — it's not cash or credit. Meal swipes are swiped directly; dining dollars are also swiped but come with a receipt showing your remaining balance.

How to sign up / change your meal plan:
Incoming freshmen are automatically enrolled in Traditional 19 — first-year students are required to have a meal plan, no exceptions. To change plans, you can go in person to the Bison One Card office (in the iLab building on Georgia Avenue) or change it yourself on the Bison Web portal under Student Services → Meal Plans. The last day to change your meal plan each semester is the second Friday of classes.

Meal swipes:
Meal swipes can be used at two dining halls: Bethune Annex (near the freshman dorms like Sutton, Carver, and the Quad) and Blackburn Cafe (on the Yard).

The two dining halls:
Bethune Annex has a pasta station, daily sandwich station, veggie bowl station, salad station, dessert station, and stir-fry station (described as consistently good). There's also a burger/grilled cheese/fries station with no line since it's made fresh on the spot, an all-day breakfast station, and an ice cream station with multiple toppings. Several stations rotate daily, and there's a "Soulful Thursday" station — described as good but can start to feel repetitive after a while.

Blackburn offers mostly the same as Annex, but with better vegan options according to the narrator; some students prefer Blackburn for that reason. Both dining halls will accommodate special diets (vegan butter, vegan cheese, gluten-free items, etc.) if you ask — options aren't always visibly displayed.

Dining dollars:
Dining dollars come with the meal plan and can be used anywhere on campus — the dining halls, the C-Store (a convenience store next to Blackburn called "Simply to Go"), a small cafe called Jasmine's (described as a Howard version of Starbucks), and at Punchout.

Punchout (food court) vendors mentioned: Wild Chicken (described very positively, but with consistently long lines — "there's literally always a line"), Pandini's (pizza and pasta), Halal Shack (described as "a Mediterranean Chipotle," with the naan and chicken; also has long lines but the narrator preferred it for its speed and quality), and NuVegan (vegan soul food, noted as possibly renovating/changing at time of recording).

Dining dollars can be reloaded in $50 increments.

Bison Bucks:
Bison Bucks are a prepaid stored-value account tied to the Bison One Card, separate from dining dollars. Family members can deposit money into it. It works for laundry machines, vending machines, on-campus food options, and select off-campus vendors including Ben's Chili Bowl, Chipotle, CVS, Whole Foods, and Starbucks.

Meal plan options if living on campus:
- Traditional 19: 19 meals/week + $250 dining dollars/semester
- Traditional 14: 14 meals/week + $300 dining dollars/semester
- Block 170: 170 meals/semester + $375 dining dollars/semester
- Block 140: 140 meals/semester + $400 dining dollars/semester (described as the cheapest option with the most dining dollars)

Meal plan options if living off campus:
- Block 75: 75 meals/semester + $450 dining dollars/semester
- Dining Dollars Only: $1,125/semester, no meal swipes

Which plan the narrator recommends:
The narrator chose Block 140 as a freshman — it was the cheapest option, had the most dining dollars, and fit an irregular eating schedule (not eating in the cafeteria twice a day consistently). She notes cafe hours on weekends are limited and shorter, so many students use Punchout (dining dollars) rather than dining-hall swipes on weekends anyway. She says she'd consider Block 170 next time for the extra 30 swipes at a cost of only $25 less in dining dollars, and recommends deciding between Block 140 and Block 170 depending on whether you prefer more dining-hall meals or more flexibility to eat elsewhere.

The C-Store:
Described as similar to a mini grocery store / small Walmart — sells toiletries, snacks, candy, drinks, and microwaveable food. There are C-Stores near Annex, next to Blackburn, and in West and East Towers (the tower locations have more apartment-style microwaveable food since those buildings have kitchens).

Tips and tricks from the narrator:
1. Set a dining-dollars budget and stick to it — many students run out of dining dollars by midterms.
2. Plan around your class schedule when eating at Punchout, since lines can take 30-60+ minutes during peak hours.
3. Visit Punchout during off-peak times, buy extra food, and refrigerate it for later (a mini-fridge is recommended for freshman dorms) rather than waiting in long lines repeatedly.
4. The "Freshman 15" is real — be mindful of how dining hall food and frequent candy/ice cream trips can add up.
5. Students with dietary needs/restrictions should contact Howard's dining office directly for accommodations.
